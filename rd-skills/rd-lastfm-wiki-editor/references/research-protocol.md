# Research protocol and evidence decisions

Use this file for any new wiki entry, substantive revision, source check, or disputed fact.

## 1. Resolve identity before gathering prose

An artist name alone is not necessarily unique. Compare the supplied artist's country/scene, dates, associated albums, labels, aliases, and known collaborators. For a track, identify the **recording** and credited artist; cover versions, remixes, remasters, live takes, and re-recordings may have different metadata. For an album, distinguish a release group/work from specific editions and countries. Keep the record label separate from any artist of the same name.

Search alternative spellings and aliases as leads but retain the subject's established name in the final wiki. Only ask a question when reasonable searches and user context cannot separate candidates.

## 2. Match sources to facts

Prefer, in general:

1. Artist/label announcements, official release pages, publisher credits, and first-party interviews about the artist's own experience.
2. Liner notes, packaging, official metadata and digital distributor/label records for the relevant **edition**.
3. MusicBrainz and Discogs entries as discovery/cross-checking aids, remembering that community-contributed metadata may be wrong.
4. Reputable journalism, interviews, archives, and historical reporting for narrative events and contextual claims.
5. Other relevant independently sourced databases or histories.

This is not a mechanical ranking: contemporaneous liner notes beat an artist's recent memory for printed credits; a firsthand interview can be strongest for intention but does not establish the factual truth of every recollection. Existing Last.fm text is only a research lead, never independent confirmation.

Check important claims against two genuinely independent sources where practical. Do not demand multiple sources when a single authoritative primary document directly establishes a routine fact, but investigate conflicts.

## 3. Claim/evidence ledger (private working notes)

Keep an informal table during research, not in the published wiki:

| Claim | Source and date | Type | Status | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Original release was on a certain date | Primary release announcement | Primary | Supported | May use |
| A recording includes a particular guest | Liner notes and label credits | Primary | Supported | May use |
| Genre label appears on one unsourced page | Informal site | Secondary | Uncertain | Avoid or corroborate |
| Two sources give different years | Independent sources | Mixed | Disputed | Investigate; qualify or omit |

Statuses: **supported**, **uncertain**, **disputed**, **contradicted**, **not checked**. Do not convert uncertainty to certainty during prose editing.

## 4. Date and credit traps

- Distinguish announcement, recording, first physical issue, digital release, regional issue, rerelease, deluxe edition, remaster, and later upload.
- Distinguish an EP from a debut studio album, a compilation from a new album, and original album credits from reissue bonus tracks.
- Separate producer, mixer, masterer, recording engineer, songwriter, composer, label, distributor, performer, and guest credits.
- Track and album artist tags must use the *actual credited artist for the linked Last.fm entry*; do not automatically use an interview subject or album compiler.
- A band's inactivity does not prove a split; an old biography's present tense does not establish current activity.
- Claims about commercial performance, genre, cultural significance, influence, or lyrical meaning require especially careful sourcing.
- Do not invent quotations, present unverified fan interpretations as intentions, or reproduce copyrighted lyrics.

## 5. Research deliverables

When the user asks for a source check, present:

- **Verified findings:** concise factual claims with actual links or citations.
- **Conflicts or limitations:** which claims remain disputed, ambiguous, or insufficiently documented.
- **Implications for the wiki:** what should be included, qualified, corrected, or excluded.

For combined research-plus-draft requests, finish the explanation before a clearly separated plain BBCode entry. If the user asked only for the wiki, use the research internally; output BBCode only.

Never manufacture source titles, links, quotations, or the appearance of having searched. If research tooling is unavailable, be explicit about the verification limit outside the wiki and draft conservatively from trustworthy information already in context.
